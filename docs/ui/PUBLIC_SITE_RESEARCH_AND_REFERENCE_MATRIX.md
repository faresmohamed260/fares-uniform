# Public site research and reference matrix

Status: **RESEARCH BASELINE / NOT A DESIGN APPROVAL, 2026-09-17.**

Owner document: [Public site redesign governance](PUBLIC_SITE_REDESIGN_GOVERNANCE.md).

This research supports the client-authorized complete redesign of the Fares Uniform public site. It combines current fashion/uniform marketing research, high-expression interactive web references, and implementation/design-engineering sources. It is a reference library, not permission to copy another brand or install every listed dependency.

No public-web source, deployment, database, provider, DNS or Gate D state is changed by this research.

## 1. Research thesis

The strongest direction for Fares Uniform is **not** “make a beautiful clothing store.” The site should present Fares as a design-and-manufacturing partner that turns an organization’s identity and operational needs into a coherent uniform program.

The best external references split into two complementary groups:

1. **fashion/interactive presentation references** — teach us how to make garments, material and process feel alive;
2. **uniform/B2B references** — teach us what information convinces a serious organization to begin a uniform project.

The redesign should combine the two rather than choosing one extreme. A pure fashion-art site can be memorable but commercially vague. A conventional uniform supplier site can be clear but visually interchangeable. Fares should aim for **high-fashion presentation discipline with B2B program clarity**.

## 2. Marketing lessons from the uniform market

### Noel Asmar Uniforms

Sources:
- https://www.noelasmaruniforms.com/
- https://www.noelasmaruniforms.com/pages/custom-uniforms

Useful lessons:
- positions the uniform as a **professional statement**, not a commodity;
- directly connects garments to the client’s **brand story**;
- explains that one program may need coherent solutions for multiple roles;
- makes performance fabric and real work conditions part of design quality;
- uses a human “uniform expert/specialist” as the conversion path rather than treating every project as self-service shopping;
- separates levels of customization so a prospect understands that “uniform program” is broader than one garment.

What Fares should borrow:
- language around identity + function + team roles;
- a visible “we design around how your team operates” proposition;
- material/performance/craft explanation when we have truthful details to publish;
- a consultation-oriented conversion path.

What Fares must not copy:
- Noel Asmar’s identity, slogans, photography, product taxonomy, claims, pricing, customer logos or exact tier structure;
- ecommerce price/shopping conventions, because Fares’ accepted public contract exposes no price or stock.

### Jermyn Street Design (JSD)

Sources:
- https://jsd.co.uk/case-studies/
- https://jsd.co.uk/restaurant-uniforms/
- representative case studies such as Nuffield Health, IHG, RAC and Coworth Park

Useful lessons:
- strong B2B case studies start with the **client problem and operational context**, not a beauty shot;
- the design story explains brand cues, staff roles, fabric/function, wearer feedback, sampling/trials and rollout;
- the uniform becomes an extension of a client rebrand and customer experience;
- operational credibility — measuring, trials, repeat orders, logistics and consistency — matters alongside visual design.

What Fares should borrow:
- future case-study structure: brief → identity/operational constraints → design choices → sample/refinement → manufacture/delivery → outcome;
- show the people/process behind the garments, not just finished catalog cards;
- explain why a design choice exists, not only what it looks like.

What Fares must not do yet:
- fabricate named case studies, client quotations, rollout sizes, countries, wearer counts, awards or outcomes;
- use synthetic staging content in a way that implies a real client engagement.

### Cargo Crew

Sources:
- https://www.cargocrew.com/about-us/
- https://global.cargocrew.com/cargo-crew-house/
- https://www.cargocrew.com/uniform-case-study-rydges/

Useful lessons:
- explicitly frames uniforms as **presentation, confidence and brand experience**;
- treats modern workwear as a meeting point between fashion and function;
- its 2026 “Cargo Crew House” turned a uniform business into a physical/runway-style design experience rather than treating workwear as visually secondary;
- role/function and wearer comfort remain part of the fashion story.

What Fares should borrow:
- confidence that a uniform company can present itself with fashion-level art direction without losing operational credibility;
- the idea that the people wearing uniforms are part of the brand experience;
- future editorial/runway content can exist alongside serious B2B conversion.

## 3. High-expression fashion and interactive references

### A. Bécane Paris — live fashion commerce with spatial product presentation

Sources:
- https://www.becaneparis.com/
- https://www.csswinner.com/details/bcane-paris/19039
- https://www.hoverstat.es/features/becane/

Observed/reference characteristics:
- current minimal collection surface with unusually large negative space and tiny full-body product figures;
- 3D frame viewers and carefully orchestrated transitions called out by HOVERSTAT.ES;
- fashion/ecommerce launch experience recognized by CSS Winner in January 2026;
- deliberately small amount of chrome around a distinctive collection presentation.

Borrow for Fares:
- product discovery does not need a conventional card grid;
- garments can occupy a shared spatial field and become the navigation object;
- transition quality can make a sparse layout feel expensive;
- “collection” may be presented as a cast/team rather than a row of product tiles.

Do not copy:
- monochrome identity, exact micro-figure composition, motorcycle narrative, ecommerce cart or collection structure.

Fares hypothesis:
- explore a “team lineup” catalog mode where uniform looks are presented as coordinated roles/silhouettes, with a stable accessible list/grid equivalent and strong mobile recomposition.

### B. Weekend Max Mara — The Holiday Edit

Sources:
- https://monogrid.com/en/project/weekend-max-mara-the-holiday-edit
- Awwwards recognition, January 2026

Observed/reference characteristics:
- transforms a seasonal product edit into a poetic interactive journey instead of a conventional catalog;
- uses a strong physical metaphor/object and CG/3D world to reveal products;
- Awwwards recognized it as Site of the Day and a January Product Honor winner.

Borrow for Fares:
- one memorable physical metaphor can carry the whole experience;
- product reveal can feel like discovery rather than scrolling past cards;
- an expressive scene can still point back to real garments.

Do not copy:
- holiday/festive identity, gift mechanic, decorative object, retail commerce flow or Max Mara art direction.

Fares hypothesis:
- find a metaphor native to Fares: fabric becoming cut panels, panels becoming garment, garments resolving into a coordinated team/program.

### C. Infinite Passerella — Lusion

Sources:
- https://exp-infinite-passerella.lusion.co/
- https://lusion.co/projects/infinite_passerella/

Observed/reference characteristics:
- an always-running, real-time virtual fashion show;
- garments and runway are the spatial hero;
- user can change view/lighting and take snapshots;
- project explicitly lists WebGL and received Awwwards/FWA Site of the Day recognition.

Borrow for Fares:
- clothing can be treated as a spatial object with presence rather than a JPEG inside a card;
- lighting/material can communicate textile character;
- optional interaction can deepen interest without replacing the underlying story.

Do not copy:
- a literal runway as the entire site, fashion-week spectacle, camera/snapshot feature or Lusion scene.

Fares hypothesis:
- a bounded 3D/product stage on landing or detail could let a uniform silhouette/material respond to scroll and pointer, while all essential content remains ordinary semantic HTML.

### D. The Hands That Sew — Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa / Sayavera

Sources:
- https://sayavera.studio/en/the-hands-that-sew-balenciaga-webdoc/
- https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/en/discover/collection-/research-/the-hands-that-sew.html

Observed/reference characteristics:
- minimalist interactive archive with contemporary editorial art direction;
- historical documents, worker portraits, film and testimony are treated as a coherent digital narrative;
- focuses on the people and craft behind fashion rather than only the famous finished object.

Borrow for Fares:
- manufacturing credibility can be emotional and editorial, not a generic “our process” icon row;
- hands, measuring, pattern work, samples, stitching and finishing can become powerful evidence if photographed truthfully later;
- strong typography + archival/detail imagery can create premium feeling without heavy 3D everywhere.

Do not copy:
- Balenciaga identity, museum archival styling, historical content or exact black/white typography.

Fares hypothesis:
- use real future workshop/process photography as tactile editorial chapters: understand → sketch/design → sample → refine → manufacture → handover. Until approved real photography exists, concepts use clearly synthetic/reference assets only.

### E. Finely Crafted — The Shoe Surgeon / Media.Monks

Sources:
- https://www.awwwards.com/sites/finely-crafted
- https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/finely-crafted
- Webby 2022 Honoree for navigation/structure

Observed/reference characteristics:
- interactive 3D tour of the maker’s studio;
- visitors move through a defined spatial path and discover story-driven hotspots;
- the experience explains how one product comes to life;
- technology listed includes WebGL and GSAP.

Borrow for Fares:
- process can become an explorable place/story instead of a list of four steps;
- constrain navigation enough to prevent people getting lost;
- manufacturing details and materials can be revealed at the moment they matter.

Do not copy:
- literal studio layout, map/honeycomb mechanic, sneaker collaboration or dark/gold brand language.

Fares hypothesis:
- the uniform program could progress through a controlled visual “workbench” sequence where the same garment/team identity changes as each phase is explained.

### F. TILToooTILT — tote inc.

Source:
- https://tiltoootilt.tote.co.jp/

Observed/reference characteristics:
- fashion objects are assigned explicit imaginary mass, force, density, drift and stability;
- the experience is built around differentiated physical response rather than identical hover animations;
- current 2026 showcase evidence describes Three.js/WebGL + GSAP + draggable inertia.

Borrow for Fares:
- physical response should reflect what an object *is*;
- fabric/swatches can feel light/soft; a garment panel can settle differently from a button or heavy jacket;
- physics is more convincing when parameters differ intentionally.

Do not copy:
- physics playground as the primary information architecture;
- constant whole-screen tilt or novelty interaction on conversion-critical controls.

Fares hypothesis:
- use very bounded material physics for a hero fabric/garment composition or swatch interaction; keep navigation/forms stable.

### G. Amanda Braga portfolio / Cappen

Source:
- https://cappen.com/work/neondoor/ (Cappen’s work index currently describes the Amanda Braga project)

Observed/reference characteristics:
- described by the studio as a “digital runway” and “editorial stage”;
- seamless scroll journey, expressive typography and fluid GSAP interaction;
- portfolio is treated as an unfolding narrative rather than a gallery index.

Borrow for Fares:
- page rhythm should feel choreographed from chapter to chapter;
- typography can be an active part of movement and hierarchy;
- transitions can connect sections rather than each section entering independently.

Do not copy:
- portfolio structure, creator identity or exact typographic treatment.

### H. Pattern Breaking — Driftime

Sources:
- https://patternbreak.ing/
- https://driftime.com/work/pattern-breaking
- https://onepagelove.com/pattern-breaking

Observed/reference characteristics:
- 2026 one-page storytelling project using scroll effects/GSAP/React;
- transforms a complex argument about deceptive fashion UI into an interactive narrative;
- deliberately exposes manipulative ecommerce patterns rather than using them.

Borrow for Fares:
- scroll can structure education and reveal, not just decorate;
- one-page storytelling can handle serious content without becoming a brochure;
- avoid fake scarcity, fake social proof, manipulative timers or conversion dark patterns.

### I. The Renaissance Edition — Shopify Editions Winter ’26

Sources:
- Awwwards 2026 recognition / February Site of the Month
- Shopify Winter ’26 interactive edition

Observed/reference characteristics:
- classical/generative-painted scenes mixed with modern commerce objects;
- multi-plane depth and hidden-layer reveal mechanics;
- demonstrates how a large body of content can feel like one art-directed world rather than a stack of sections.

Borrow for Fares:
- create a consistent visual world and reuse it across chapters;
- hidden layers/masks can reveal construction/material/process detail;
- an information-heavy story can still feel cinematic.

Do not copy:
- Renaissance imagery, Shopify identity, novelty easter eggs or product-update structure.

### J. BOSS Dubai Experience / Lacoste Ace Breaker

Sources:
- BOSS Dubai Experience award/case pages (2024)
- https://members-play.lacoste.com/ace-breaker/gb/en/ (2026 campaign)

Why keep them in the library:
- BOSS shows a product capsule embedded in a 360-degree place/atmosphere;
- Lacoste shows that a fashion brand can build a fully playable campaign rather than a webpage.

Fares relevance:
- useful as ceiling references for immersion and interaction production quality;
- not direct templates for Fares. A business-uniform prospect should not have to play a game or navigate a virtual venue to understand capability or enquire.

## 4. Candidate Fares interaction territories

These are **research hypotheses, not approved concepts**.

### Territory 1 — From material to team

Core idea:
- a tactile fabric/pattern object enters as the first visual;
- scroll transforms the same object through cut panels/sample/garment;
- the garment multiplies/recomposes into a coordinated team lineup;
- the lineup becomes the entry point to programs/catalog;
- selection carries the same visual object into detail.

Why it fits:
- directly expresses what the business does;
- gives GSAP/Motion/WebGL a clear purpose;
- supports fashion-level presentation without inventing a story unrelated to uniforms.

### Territory 2 — The uniform workbench

Core idea:
- the site behaves like an editorial design/manufacturing workbench;
- sample cards, swatches, sketches, garment views and production detail occupy one spatial canvas;
- as the visitor scrolls, the same project evolves from brief to sample to delivered team.

Why it fits:
- maps directly to Fares’ actual client workflow;
- can combine the Balenciaga “hands/craft” sensibility with Finely Crafted’s process-as-place model;
- gives large clients a reason to trust the process.

### Territory 3 — Team as collection

Core idea:
- instead of ecommerce cards, each program is introduced as a coordinated cast of roles/silhouettes;
- visitors move across the team, then isolate a role/look/detail;
- on narrow/touch, this becomes a strong horizontal/vertical editorial rail with no essential gesture-only behavior.

Why it fits:
- uniforms are about teams, not isolated products;
- closest link to Bécane’s sparse spatial collection and runway presentation without copying either.

### Territory 4 — Quiet editorial craft

Core idea:
- no heavy 3D as the defining feature;
- oversized editorial typography, extreme crop photography, material close-ups, sticky narrative frames and elegant shared transitions;
- a single signature hero/product transition provides the kinetic identity.

Why it fits:
- gives us a premium fallback direction if 3D/WebGL adds more cost than value;
- still satisfies the request for a presentation-like living site through composition and motion rather than spectacle.

## 5. Tool and design-engineering research

### Taste Skill — Leonxlnx/taste-skill

Sources:
- https://www.tasteskill.dev/
- https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill

Useful rules:
- read the brief/audience before choosing an aesthetic;
- declare a design read instead of defaulting to a generic AI visual language;
- tune design variance, motion intensity and visual density intentionally;
- explicitly rejects common AI defaults such as centered hero + dark mesh, three equal cards, purple glow and generic glass;
- audit first on redesigns and require a strict pre-flight pass;
- isolate interactive Motion/scroll/pointer physics into client leaves rather than turning the whole React tree into an animation state machine.

Provisional Fares interpretation for concept work:
- landing: high variance / high motion / low-to-medium density;
- catalog/detail: high visual authorship, moderate-high motion, low density;
- enquiry: lower motion and moderate density for trust/readability.

Do **not** treat the Taste Skill’s numerical defaults, preferred icon family or font list as Fares decisions. They are heuristics, not project authority.

### Web Taste — lllkn/web-taste (secondary workflow reference)

Source:
- https://github.com/lllkn/web-taste

Why it is useful:
- “evidence before opinion”;
- real rendered desktop/mobile/interaction/motion inspection;
- a Visual Taste Board before saving preferences;
- only explicitly approved relationships become reusable memory;
- learns hierarchy/rhythm/proportion/component grammar rather than copying source identity/assets/code.

This strongly matches the Fares reference-matrix and approval workflow.

### Vercel Web Interface Guidelines

Sources:
- https://vercel.com/design/guidelines
- https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines

Use as an implementation QA checklist, especially:
- semantic elements, labels and accessible names;
- visible `:focus-visible` and focus not obscured by sticky UI;
- correct form types/input modes/autocomplete and inline actionable errors;
- never block paste;
- honor reduced motion and make animations interruptible;
- avoid `transition: all`;
- image dimensions, lazy/priority rules and still fallbacks for decorative video;
- URL/deep-link semantics where state matters;
- gesture alternatives for keyboard/tap;
- safe areas and overflow;
- locale-aware formatting and no IP-based language assumptions.

These are baseline quality rules, not the visual concept.

### Awesome DESIGN.md — VoltAgent/awesome-design-md

Source:
- https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md

Useful lesson:
- design authority can be captured in plain Markdown with explicit atmosphere, semantic colors, typography, components, layout, elevation, do/don’t rules, responsive behavior and agent guidance;
- the collection contains extracted systems from many well-known sites (including Nike, Apple, Bugatti, Ferrari, Lamborghini, BMW and others).

Fares use:
- treat the format as a documentation/checklist reference;
- after a concept is actually approved, consider distilling the accepted public site into a compact Fares-owned DESIGN.md-style specification so implementation agents do not reinterpret it.

Do not copy another company’s DESIGN.md into Fares as the source of truth.

### Image-to-code workflow

Current Product Design / image-to-code principle researched:
- only use after a visual target is selected;
- treat the approved image/mock as a specification, not loose inspiration;
- catalog real assets and measure layout/geometry;
- identify matching fonts/icons rather than approximating them casually;
- implement responsive/interaction states explicitly;
- compare browser output against the visual target and block handoff on meaningful fidelity defects.

Fares use:
- useful **after** we choose a full visual concept;
- not a substitute for marketing strategy, reference research or human design approval.

### Emil Kowalski design-engineering skills

Source:
- https://emilkowal.ski/skill

Relevant tools/principles:
- `animate` for choosing curve/duration/properties deliberately;
- `review-animations` for strict motion QA;
- `find-animation-opportunities` for deciding what genuinely benefits from motion and what should stay still;
- `prototype` for building genuinely different UI variants and comparing them visually;
- animation vocabulary to describe physical response precisely.

Fares use:
- especially valuable for the interaction-choreography + kinetic-prototype stage;
- use it to reduce motion clutter, not to justify adding animation everywhere.

### Lenis

Sources:
- https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis
- https://lenis.darkroom.engineering/

Current project description:
- lightweight smooth-scroll layer built around native scroll;
- designed for WebGL scroll synchronization, parallax and integration with GSAP/ScrollTrigger;
- React adapter available.

Fares policy:
- **candidate, not default**;
- adopt only if the selected concept needs scroll/3D synchronization and browser evidence shows it improves the experience;
- ordinary navigation, anchors, keyboard/touch behavior and reduced-motion mode remain complete without the smoothing effect.

### GSAP / ScrollTrigger

Source:
- https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/

Relevant capabilities:
- trigger, scrub, pin, snap and coordinate timelines against scroll;
- responsive setups and rich lifecycle callbacks;
- strong fit for chapter-based scroll stories, image sequences, SVG/mask choreography and coordinated pinned scenes.

Fares policy:
- use where a selected signature interaction genuinely needs timeline/scroll orchestration;
- not a reason to animate every section;
- isolate the runtime, support reduced motion, keep pinning from trapping content/focus and verify mobile performance.

### Motion Primitives — ibelick/motion-primitives

Sources:
- https://motion-primitives.com/
- https://github.com/ibelick/motion-primitives

Current state:
- Motion + Tailwind animated UI kit;
- MIT licensed;
- project describes itself as beta.

Fares use:
- strong candidate for maintained morphing/dialog/disclosure/text/transition mechanics when a mechanic matches an approved interaction;
- wrap/normalize into Fares tokens and accessibility expectations;
- do not treat beta examples as a frozen API or a visual system to copy wholesale.

### React Bits — DavidHDev/react-bits

Sources:
- https://reactbits.dev/
- https://github.com/DavidHDev/react-bits

Current state:
- 165+ animated/interactive React components in the repository’s current description;
- includes text, backgrounds, UI and creative tools;

Fares use:
- excellent R&D/mechanic source for typography, galleries, image transitions and selected spatial effects;

### Watermelon UI

Sources:
- https://ui.watermelon.sh/
- https://github.com/WatermelonCorp/watermelon-platform

Current state:
- React/TypeScript/Tailwind/shadcn-based registry;

## 7. What the first concept round should intentionally test

Produce **three genuinely different full directions**, not three color variants:

1. **Material → Team** — highest kinetic/3D ambition; fabric/garment transformation as the signature idea.
2. **Editorial Workbench** — strongest craft/manufacturing story; tactile collage/workbench with elegant motion and one spatial hero.
3. **Quiet Fashion Precision** — minimal, high-typography, photography-first direction with one exceptional shared-element/product transition rather than persistent effects.

Every direction must show:
- full landing narrative, not hero only;
- catalog discovery;
- detail/program page;
- enquiry conversion;
- desktop and narrow/mobile;
- representative Arabic RTL;
- reduced-motion equivalent;
- loading/degraded-media state if 3D/video is involved;
- no price or stock affordance;
- no invented client proof.

## 8. Marketing information hierarchy to carry into concepting

The visitor should understand, in roughly this order:

1. **Fares creates uniforms around the identity and real work of a team.**
2. **This is a program, not one generic shirt:** multiple roles/looks can stay visually coherent.
3. **There is a real design-to-delivery process:** understand → design → sample/refine → manufacture → handover.
4. **Material, fit and daily use matter.**
5. **Fares can work across the accepted target sectors** without pretending every sector needs the same garment.
6. **There is a clear next step:** begin a project/enquiry or contact directly.

Catalog browsing supports the story rather than taking over the first viewport as ecommerce merchandise.

## 9. Research conclusions

- The request for a “presentation coming to life” is achievable without turning the site into a game.
- The highest-value signature interaction is likely a **garment/material transformation or continuity system**, because it is native to the product and can carry across landing → catalog → detail.
- **Craft/process storytelling** is just as important as 3D. The Balenciaga and Shoe Surgeon references show that explaining who/how something is made can feel more premium than decorative effects.
- A uniform brand can legitimately use fashion-level art direction; Cargo Crew’s 2026 immersive showcase and Noel Asmar/JSD’s brand-led program framing support that positioning.
- GSAP/Lenis/WebGL are best treated as optional production tools selected by an approved interaction, not as requirements.
- Motion Primitives, React Bits and Watermelon UI are mechanic/component sources; none should define Fares’ visual identity.
- Taste Skill, Web Taste, Vercel guidelines, Emil Kowalski and image-to-code are most valuable as **process/QA systems**.
- Kaikei.app is not currently verified and should not influence design decisions until a trustworthy source exists.

Next gate: create three full visual directions and the accompanying interaction choreography. No production implementation begins until the client selects a direction and the signature kinetic behavior has reviewable temporal evidence.
